# Data Quality Findings: Raw Olist Data

- **Source:** [data-profile.md](data-profile.md) (generated by `scripts/profile_raw.py`)
- **Reviewed:** 2026-11-10

This document records what we found in the raw data, why it matters, and what
we decided to do about it. Each decision becomes a dbt model rule or test.

## Summary

The core transactional data is clean. Orders, order items, payments,
customers, products and sellers all have unique, non-null keys, and every
item, payment and review links to an existing order. The issues below are
concentrated in reviews, geolocation, product categories and customer
identity.

## Findings

### DQ-1: `review_id` is not unique (814 duplicate rows)

- **What:** Some reviews are attached to more than one order.
- **Risk:** Treating `review_id` as a key would fail tests or drop reviews.
- **Decision:** The grain of reviews is one row per `review_id` + `order_id`.
- **Test:** `unique` on the combination in the staging model.

### DQ-2: Geolocation has ~50 rows per zip prefix (1,000,163 rows, 19,015 prefixes)

- **What:** The same zip prefix appears many times with slightly different coordinates.
- **Risk:** Joining customers or sellers directly to this table multiplies rows
  ~50×, silently inflating every metric. **This is the highest-risk finding.**
- **Decision:** Aggregate to one row per zip prefix (average latitude/longitude)
  before any join.
- **Test:** `unique` on zip prefix in the aggregated model.

### DQ-3: Missing and untranslated product categories

- **What:** 610 products have no category. 13 products have a category with no
  English translation.
- **Risk:** Category reports drop or mislabel those products.
- **Decision:** Use the English name, fall back to the Portuguese name, and
  label missing values `unknown`. Never drop rows.
- **Test:** `not_null` on the final category column.

### DQ-4: Zip prefixes missing from geolocation (278 customers, 7 sellers)

- **What:** Fewer than 0.3% of customers and sellers have no coordinates.
- **Risk:** Minor; those points are missing from maps.
- **Decision:** Accept and keep the rows with null coordinates. Document on map visuals.
- **Test:** None (known, accepted gap).

### DQ-5: `customer_id` is per order, not per person

- **What:** 99,441 `customer_id`s map to 96,096 `customer_unique_id`s.
- **Risk:** Counting `customer_id` overstates customers and hides repeat purchases.
- **Decision:** The customer dimension is keyed on `customer_unique_id`.
  `customer_id` is used only to join orders to customers.
- **Test:** `unique` on `customer_unique_id` in `dim_customers`.

### DQ-6: Sparse months at both ends of the date range

- **What:** Sep–Dec 2016 and Sep–Oct 2018 have very few orders, and Nov 2016 has none.
- **Risk:** Trend charts show misleading spikes and drops.
- **Decision:** Dashboards default to Jan 2017 – Aug 2018. The full data stays in the models.
- **Test:** None; this is a reporting choice, documented on the dashboard.

### DQ-7: Payments with type `not_defined` (3 rows, value 0)

- **What:** Three payments have an undefined method and zero value.
- **Risk:** Negligible financially; it clutters payment-type breakdowns.
- **Decision:** Keep the rows. The `accepted_values` test lists `not_defined`
  explicitly, so any new unexpected type fails.
- **Test:** `accepted_values` on `payment_type`.

### DQ-8: Geolocation points outside Brazil (42 rows, 21 zip prefixes)

- **What:** Some coordinates fall far outside Brazil (latitudes up to 45°, longitudes up to 121°).
- **Risk:** Averaging them into a zip's location moves it off the map.
- **Decision:** Exclude points outside Brazil's bounding box (lat −34 to 5.3,
  lng −74 to −34.7) before averaging. 5 zips have no valid point left and
  drop out, so their customers/sellers get null coordinates (same as DQ-4).
- **Test:** Enforced in `int_geolocation__by_zip`. Found during Phase 4 design.
